The Story of Sowala

Sowala began with a simple act of love.

For years, a therapist dedicated to helping children on the autism spectrum would call her husband while he was out and ask, Can you pick up another sensory toy for one of my kids? More often than not, she was looking for something soft, comforting, and especially a sloth, because her clients loved holding and cuddling them.

Her husband began noticing how much these simple toys meant to the children. They weren’t just stuffed animals—they could provide comfort, security, and a calming sensory experience.

That inspired an idea: What if he could create a sensory companion designed specifically with these children in mind?

And that’s how Sowala was born—a soft, lovable sensory friend designed with different textures to touch and explore, comforting weight to hold close, and a removable warm‑or‑cool insert for an added soothing experience.

Sowala was inspired by a therapist, created with love, and made to bring comfort—one hug at a time.

The system

One companion, four kinds of comfort.

Every Sowala combines four sensory functions in one plush. All inserts are removable, so the outer animal goes in the washing machine.

Weight

A removable weighted pouch sits in the lower belly. Deep, even pressure—a hug that stays. Double‑sealed inner pouch with poly pellets, not reachable in normal use; take it out to wash.

Prototype target: Large 3 lb · Small 2 lb

Warm & Cool

A second pocket holds a separate insert. Chill the reusable gel pack in the fridge, or warm the microwaveable grain insert made for plush—heated by the guide, checked by an adult. No wires, no batteries.

What’s inside

Two pockets, two inserts, one washable plush.

An exploded view of the lower belly. The weighted pouch and the thermal insert live in separate pockets behind one hidden flap—so each can be removed, replaced or left out on its own.

  1. 1
    Outer plush

    The animal itself: long‑pile or curly fur, embroidered face, no loose parts. With both inserts removed it goes in the washing machine.

  2. 2
    Hidden belly flap

    One fur‑covered opening on the lower belly, closed with a concealed, child‑resistant hook‑and‑loop flap under the seam. It is not obvious to a child in normal play and needs an adult’s deliberate pull—no zipper a child could open and climb into, and nothing that lets a child reach the pellets or the gel pack.

  3. 3
    Pocket B · warm‑or‑cool insert

    The upper pocket takes either the reusable gel pack straight from the fridge or the microwaveable grain insert (flaxseed‑type, made for plush). The insert is only as warm as the guide and the adult make it—so the heating guide is printed on it. No electronics anywhere in the toy.

  4. 4
    Pocket A · weighted pouch

    The lower‑belly pocket holds a double‑sealed pouch of poly pellets, so the weight sits low and the animal settles into the lap. Prototype target 3 lb (Large) / 2 lb (Small), to be tuned in testing. It looks nothing like the thermal insert and is tagged Do not microwave—poly pellets melt.

  5. 5
    Divider seam

    A stitched wall between the two pockets. Thermal and weighted materials never share a pouch—easier to make, clean, replace and test.

Why two pockets and not one? Keeping the thermal insert separate from the weighting material makes manufacturing, cleaning, replacement and safety review much simpler—each insert can be swapped or sold as a spare on its own.

How it works

Chill or warm. Slide in. Hug.

Three steps, all done by an adult. The weighted pouch stays in for everyday play; the thermal insert goes in when it’s wanted.

Step 1

Chill or warm the insert

Gel pack in the fridge for cool; the grain insert in the microwave for warm—by the printed guide, in short bursts, from room temperature. An adult tests it on their own skin first. Only the grain insert ever goes in the microwave.

Step 2

Slide it into the belly pocket

Open the hidden flap, slide the insert into its own pocket above the weighted pouch, and press the flap closed.

Step 3

Hug

The weight settles low in the lap, the insert warms or cools the belly, and the long arms wrap around. Awake and supervised—never left alone under the weight, never over the face.

Safety & positioning

Say who it’s for. Never promise what it will do.

Sowala names its audience and tells its true origin story. It does not make health claims—a children’s product that involves weight and a heated component has to be described carefully and tested properly.

What we say

  • Weighted sensory companion
  • Warm & cool comfort insert
  • Multiple tactile textures
  • Designed for sensory exploration and comfort
  • Calming deep‑pressure feel · cozy, soothing, grounding
  • Designed with children on the autism spectrum in mind
  • Inspired by a therapist who works with children on the autism spectrum
  • Sensory‑friendly · a sensory friend

“Calming” always describes the feel of the weight, never the child. The therapist inspired it—that is the only way the word appears.

What we never say

  • That it treats, helps, relieves, reduces or improves autism, ADHD, anxiety, meltdowns, stress, pain, sleep, focus or sensory processing
  • Therapeutic, clinical, medical, doctor‑recommended, therapist‑approved, hot/cold therapy
  • Proven, scientifically, clinically, temperature‑limited
  • Any sleep claim—it is not a sleep product
  • “Helps with anxiety”, “calms meltdowns”, “regulates”, “may help”—a hedge is still read as a claim
  • “10 % of body weight” as if it were evidence
  • That CPSC “approves” or “regulates” weighted plush—there is no such rule

The FTC’s Health Products Compliance Guidance (2022) treats any health‑related claim on any product—express or implied—as needing competent and reliable scientific evidence, generally randomized controlled trials. Testimonials don’t count. Saying who it is designed for is allowed; saying what it will do to them is not.

The heating guide that goes on the insert
  • Only the grain insert goes in the microwave—never the plush, never the weighted pouch (poly pellets melt and burn).
  • Heat from room temperature only, by the printed time and wattage (Warmies‑type flaxseed: max 90 s at 600–800 W, 60 s at 850–1000 W), in 5–20 s bursts.
  • An adult heats it, stays at the microwave, and tests it on their own skin before the child gets it.
  • Never reheat until it is fully cool; never heat with a blocked turntable; stop if it smells scorched.
  • For cool: gel pack in the fridge, or the grain insert in a sealed bag in the freezer for 2–3 hours.
  • Inspect the gel pack for leaks before each use and discard it if damaged.

Regulatory checklist (U.S.)

  1. ASTM F963‑23 — the mandatory toy standardASTM F963 is mandatory under CPSIA §106 and codified at 16 CFR 1250 for toys for children under 14. The current edition is F963‑23; the CPSC rule adopting it took effect April 20, 2024, so anything made after that date is tested to F963‑23. For a stuffed toy that means seam strength (pellets and stuffing must not escape), stuffing cleanliness, bite / drop / torque / tension / compression use‑and‑abuse tests, and cords and elastics.
  2. A children’s product, third‑party testedMarketed for kids, Sowala is a “children’s product” (designed primarily for ages 12 and under, 16 CFR 1200.2) whoever pays for it. It must be tested by a CPSC‑accepted, ISO/IEC 17025‑accredited laboratory and retested periodically (at least annually for most firms), and the maker or importer issues a written Children’s Product Certificate under CPSA §14 before the first sale.
  3. Lead & phthalatesAccessible parts: total lead ≤ 100 ppm; paint and coatings ≤ 90 ppm. No more than 0.1 % of any of eight phthalates—DEHP, DBP, BBP, DINP, DIBP, DPENP, DHEXP, DCHP (16 CFR 1307)—which matters for any vinyl or plasticized film such as a gel‑pack skin.
  4. Small parts & age grading16 CFR 1501 bans small parts in toys for under‑3s, including parts freed in use‑and‑abuse testing; a toy for ages 3–6 that has a small part carries the fixed 16 CFR 1500.19 choking warning. CPSC judges the intended age by labeling, marketing, consumer recognition and its 2020 Age Determination Guidelines—ordinary plush is listed as fine for infants—so “Ages 3+” has to be earned by the weight and the inserts, not by the label alone.
  5. Magnets: none, and the right ruleFor toys the magnet rule is ASTM F963 §4.38—no hazardous magnet (a small object with a flux index ≥ 50 kG²·mm²) in the toy or liberated from it after use‑and‑abuse—not 16 CFR 1262, which excludes toys. Hook‑and‑loop closures keep Sowala out of that section and out of the ingestion hazard altogether.
  6. Closures & entrapmentTarget’s 2022 Pillowfort weighted‑blanket recall (≈ 204,000 units, two children aged 4 and 6 died) was a zipper a child could open and climb inside. Sowala’s single flap is concealed and child‑resistant, sized so a child cannot open the pocket or reach the pellets or the gel pack; F963 seam and use‑and‑abuse tests check that it holds.
  7. Weighted items & sleepThe 2024 AAP and CPSC warnings and retailer withdrawals concern weighted infant sleep products—blankets, sacks, swaddles. There is no CPSC rule on weighted products for children, and none was in progress at the time of writing (confirm on cpsc.gov before print). Sowala is an awake, supervised companion: not for infants, not a sleep product, never over the face, never left on a child alone (a 2008 Quebec coroner’s case involved an unattended child wrapped in a weighted blanket).
  8. Heat: the hazard is over‑timingGrain heat packs have caused burns needing surgery and house fires when heated far past the guide or with a blocked turntable; “no wires, no batteries” is not “no heat hazard”. No consumer plush insert is truly temperature‑limited—self‑limiting warmth is phase‑change‑material research, not a stock part—so the guide above is printed on the insert, and the weighted pouch is unmistakably different and tagged “Do not microwave”.
  9. FlammabilityF963 requires the plush fabrics to meet 16 CFR 1610 (Class 1 normal flammability) and the stuffed toy to pass 16 CFR 1500.44. Long‑pile fur, sherpa and the curly mane are exactly the fabrics that need this test.
  10. Tracking labelCPSIA §103: a permanent tracking label on the plush, its packaging and each insert—manufacturer or private labeler, production location and date, and batch—so a single insert can be traced or recalled.
  11. Claims: FTC & FDAExpress and implied health claims need competent and reliable scientific evidence (objective tests by qualified experts, generally RCTs); hedges like “may help” don’t cure them. Disease claims also change a product’s intended use toward an FDA medical device—Gravity Blanket dropped its treatment claims in 2017 for that reason. And the best trial in autistic children (Gringras 2014, about 70 children) found a weighted blanket did not improve sleep, even though children and parents preferred it. So Sowala says “calming deep‑pressure feel” and “comfort”—never an outcome.

Manufacturing notes

Built to be washed, refilled and repaired.

Materials

  • Outer: long‑pile plush (Sloth) or short plush with curly‑pile mane (Red Panda), embroidered eyes and nose—no hard eyes.
  • Texture patches: minky, corduroy, satin, sherpa, raised‑dot fabric, waffle knit—all sewn in, none glued.
  • Weighting: poly pellets in a double‑sealed inner bag, then a fabric outer pouch.
  • Thermal: microwaveable grain insert (flaxseed‑type, made for plush), heating guide printed on it. Cool: non‑toxic gel pack in puncture‑resistant, phthalate‑compliant film—or the same grain insert chilled in the freezer.

Closures

  • One hidden belly flap with wide, soft hook‑and‑loop under a fur‑covered seam—concealed and child‑resistant, so a child cannot open the pocket.
  • No magnets, no zipper pulls, no buttons or beads.
  • Sloth arm fastening: child‑safe hook‑and‑loop at the claws, sized so a child can pull free. Arms are stuffed, not wired—no armature, nothing posable, no wire ends to expose in testing.

Washability

  • Both inserts out → outer plush is machine‑washable on a gentle cycle, air dry.
  • Weighted pouch: wipe clean. Gel pack: wipe clean, inspect for leaks. Grain insert: spot‑clean only—never in the washer or dryer—and sold as a replacement part when it wears.

Spare inserts

  • Weighted pouch, gel pack and microwave insert each sold separately as spares and replacements.
  • One insert size fits both Large and Small pockets; only the pellet weight differs.
  • The two inserts differ in color, shape and label so they cannot be confused; the weighted pouch and the plush are tagged “Do not microwave”.
